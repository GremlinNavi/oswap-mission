# OSWAP Mission Scope

This document defines the mission-level scope of OSWAP. Individual repositories may implement only a subset of this scope.

## In scope

OSWAP may include research, standards, documentation, and reference implementations concerning:

- accessible human-to-system command and workflow interfaces;
- deterministic parsing and validation around human-readable or AI-assisted input;
- consent, authorization, effect classification, and independent verification;
- provenance, accountability records, privacy-minimized attribution, and integrity evidence;
- local-first AI-assisted research and coding tools;
- multilingual terminology, localization, and region-specific presentation;
- software preservation, reproducibility, archival packaging, and verified replication;
- repository and transport abstraction across multiple compatible hosting systems;
- provider-, model-, and host-agnostic interfaces; and
- open documentation of experimental syntax, workflows, and governance models.

## Out of scope by mission

OSWAP does not exist to:

- bypass provider permissions, authentication, access controls, or host operating-system security;
- treat raw LLM output as automatically executable authorization;
- provide covert access to another person's accounts, devices, or data;
- function as a cryptocurrency, token economy, or proof-of-work system by default;
- act as a legal identity authority, certificate authority, court, regulator, or government registry;
- claim that integrity evidence proves the factual truth of underlying content;
- require compulsory publication of legal names or unnecessary sensitive personal data; or
- make one commercial hosting platform, AI provider, or national deployment the exclusive definition of OSWAP.

## Experimental work

Research prototypes may explore ideas beyond stable OSWAP behavior. Experimental work should be clearly marked and should not be represented as implemented, safe, standardized, certified, or production-ready merely because it appears in an OSWAP repository.

## Relationship to technical specifications

Mission-level scope does not replace technical specifications. Grammar, schemas, protocol behavior, conformance requirements, and implementation status belong in the repositories that define or implement those components.
